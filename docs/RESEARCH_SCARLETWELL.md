# Research: ScarletWell today, comparable platforms, evidence, and what to build

Written 2026-10-11 for the Rutgers Health Hack 2026 team. Every number carries its source URL. Where the evidence is vendor-reported, small, or unverified, it says so. Nothing here overrides `docs/SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`; where this file disagrees with it, that is flagged in section 1.

## 1. What ScarletWell offers today

ScarletWell is a Rutgers New Brunswick program for "79,000+ students, faculty, and staff," created from a 2023 task force, led since July 2024 by Peggy Swarbrick as director with Amy Spagnolo as senior program coordinator, with Joshua Langberg as Chief Wellness Officer (https://newbrunswick.rutgers.edu/scarletwell/about; https://newbrunswick.rutgers.edu/chancellor/communications/inaugural-chief-wellness-officer-scarletwell-director).

The public offer, in the program's own numbers: "65 Peer Support Leader Digital Badges Awarded," "42 Wellness at Work and Connection Grants Funded," "2,300 Participants in Grant Funded Activities," "34 Scarlet Well Wellness & Self-Care Trainings/Workshops," "1,622 Participants Reached with Trainings" (https://newbrunswick.rutgers.edu/scarletwell). These are self-reported program counts, not evaluations.

The inventory. The Wellness Inventory is a 54-item checklist across eight dimensions. The validation paper (Swarbrick et al., Psychiatric Services 2025;76:263-269, DOI 10.1176/appi.ps.20230622) used N=3,446 students, faculty and behavioral health providers and found "a one-factor model comprising 54 items," so dimensions are a presentation frame, not validated subscales (https://exa.ai/library/publication/fgsxrbg0dxp; https://www.rutgers.edu/news/wellness-inventory-rutgers-researchers-receives-empirically-valid-gold-star). The online form ("Wellness in 8 D Inventory") asks about "the past 30 days" and is used by permission of Swarbrick and CSPNJ (https://njms-web.njms.rutgers.edu/njms_wellness/public/wellness/index.php?action=quiz). In January 2025 Swarbrick said "We're building an app" (same Rutgers news URL).

The MyRutgers widget. The public description is the September 22, 2025 ScarletWell announcement: "the ScarletWell Widget is now live on my.rutgers.edu! This new tool puts wellness at your fingertips, giving our Rutgers–New Brunswick community easy access to: The Wellness Calendar – discover events, programs, and opportunities happening across campus. Resources that support the emotional, environmental, financial, intellectual, occupational, physical, social, and spiritual dimensions of wellness" (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rutgers-scarletwell_new-scarletwell-widget-now-available-on-activity-7375953188861988864-bPIV). The ComManTel 2025 paper describes it as "an interactive digital interface integrated within the Rutgers University portal" that "streamlines access to wellness resources and events through an intuitive, centralized platform" (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11368432, abstract quoted at https://newbrunswick.rutgers.edu/scarletwell/about). Flag for the team: no public source says the widget itself runs the inventory. The SOURCE_OF_TRUTH one-liner ("an 18-item quiz on MyRutgers") is not supported by what I could fetch; rule 9 of the same file already forbids claiming the widget runs the inventory, so the slide copy should say "a calendar and links widget plus a separate 54-item form." I could not log in to the portal to check the live widget.

The successor project. PULSE8 (August 2026) is "a mobile application that integrates ChatGPT with wellness habits"; "Every 30 days, users answer a series of questions across these dimensions" and get "concise (two-four sentences) personalized feedback" from "deterministic rule checks" plus structured prompts (https://soe.rutgers.edu/news/ece-grad-student-keeping-his-finger-pulse-innovation). So the 30-day, all-items cadence is the thing ScarletWell's own next step keeps, and the thing we change.

Events, peers, grants. The Wellness Calendar is cross-campus and accepts submissions (https://newbrunswick.rutgers.edu/events/scarletwell; https://www.rutgers.edu/news/how-scarletwell-redefining-personal-wellness-rutgers-and-world). Peer Support Leaders are staff and faculty, not students: a 3-hour training plus 9 mentoring sessions earns a digital badge; 40 finished in June 2025 and 22 more in 2026 (https://newbrunswick.rutgers.edu/scarletwell/about). Wellness Connection Grants (student-led, faculty-sponsored) and Wellness at Work Grants range "$200–$1,000" for September 2026 to April 30, 2027 (same URL); the first student wave averaged $1,200 (Rutgers news URL above). Grant teams must "design, implement, and evaluate" and report, which is where an aggregate view would land.

## 2. Comparable platforms and what they teach

YOU at College (Grit Digital Health). A personalized portal with content in "Succeed, Thrive, Matter" domains, about 900 resources at launch (https://wcet.wiche.edu/frontiers/2018/09/21/you-at-college/). The only outcome is vendor-reported: 81% of surveyed CSU first-years said it helped them manage stress, no sample size or control (https://edcircuit.com/grit-digital-health-launches-student-wellness-portal-you/). Campus count "more than 180" is from a sister agency blog, undated (https://sharpideas.cactusinc.com/digital-health-trends-gen-z). Lesson: personalization by self-report is the model that sold, but there is no independent evidence it changes anything.

Nod (Grit and Hopelab). Pilot RCT, N=221 first-years, four weeks: buffered loneliness and depressive symptoms in students who were vulnerable at baseline; no effect on anxiety; authors flagged engagement as the open problem (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7609198/). Lesson: small social "next steps" work for the people who need them most, which argues for our one-step, one-place card.

TimelyCare. Vendor-sponsored outcomes study of 7,064 students at 270 schools: 76% reliable improvement or remission after three or more sessions, no comparison group (https://timelycare.com/peer-reviewed-study-confirms-effectiveness-and-equity-of-virtual-psychotherapy-delivered-through-timelycare/). Retention lift of 1.3 points is a modeled estimate (https://timelycare.com/lets-talk/). Lesson: measurement-based outcomes are the currency administrators buy; our aggregate view should produce a number per term.

Mantra Health. All figures are company-reported (83% of severely depressed students improved; 70% said it helped them stay in school) (https://mantrahealth.com/post/mantra-health-patient-nps-survey-virtual-mental-health-care/). Montana Tech reported about 100 users (https://m.mtech.edu/news/2023/11/students-encouraged-to-utilize-free-mantra-health-services-for-mental-wellness.html). Lesson: utilization is the failure mode of bought platforms.

Togetherall. UTEP case study: 92% of users were not seeking help elsewhere (https://togetherall.com/en-us/case-studies/the-university-of-texas-at-el-paso-reaching-more-and-different-students-through-clinically-moderated-digital-peer-to-peer-support/). The one independent RCT of its predecessor (Big White Wall, N=790) found no difference versus an NHS information site and only 16.6% completed the primary outcome; the authors blamed a failure to "personally engage participants and explain how these unfamiliar interventions might benefit them" (https://www.jmir.org/2021/4/e23487/). Lesson: an unexplained tool is not used.

Headspace and Calm. Headspace's BU case study reports 91% of joiners returned after first use (vendor) (https://get.headspace.com/boston-university-case-study). A Georgia Tech study found students answered 76% of prompts over two weeks with steady use (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12442982/). Notre Dame paid $150,000 for Calm; about 3,000 of its students signed up and 2,000 engaged, and students criticized the spend (https://ndsmcobserver.com/2021/02/university-sponsored-calm-app-subscriptions-met-with-mixed-student-responses/). Lesson: bought subscriptions reach a minority and draw criticism; a tool tied to campus places does something a subscription cannot.

JED Campus. 2013 to 2023 data from 136 schools: students at completing schools were 25% less likely to report a suicide attempt, 13% suicide planning, 10% ideation, relative differences from JED's own report (https://jedfoundation.org/jed-campus-impact-report-2024/; https://jedfoundation.org/new-report-documents-lower-suicidal-ideation-suicidal-planning-and-attempts-on-college-campuses-implementing-the-jed-foundations-comprehensive-mental-health-approach/). Lesson: system-level, dose-response work beats any app; our staff view should be framed as an input to that kind of plan.

Healthy Minds utilization. 2024-25, 84,000+ students at 135 schools: moderate to severe depression 37%, anxiety 32%, high loneliness 52%, flourishing 36% (down from 38%), therapy 37%, medication 30%, and only 60% of students with positive screens got treatment; top barriers were time (23%), money (22%), and preferring to handle it alone (18%) (https://sph.umich.edu/news/2025posts/college-student-mental-health-third-consecutive-year-improvement.html; report PDF https://healthymindsnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2024-2025_HMS-National-Data-Report_Student.pdf). Lesson: the gap is not awareness, it is time and fit, which supports a two-minute check-in and a nearby place.

Ohio State: Wellness app. Launched January 2020 with four tabs (Explore, Support, My Wellness, Crisis), pushed to 23,000 student iPads; no adoption numbers published since (https://news.osu.edu/ohio-state-wellness-app-puts-tools-resources-in-student-hands/; https://it.osu.edu/node/3030). Penn State offers WellTrack Boost through CAPS with no public usage data (https://studentaffairs.psu.edu/health-wellbeing/mental-health-services/self-care/welltrack-boost). Michigan's Wolverine Wellness is coaching and courses, intake by a 5-minute survey, no published outcomes (https://wolverinewellness.umich.edu/wellness-coaching). Lesson: campus-built apps ship and go quiet; without a feedback loop to staff they have no owner.

Digital twin and passive sensing pilots. StudentLife (Dartmouth, 48 students, one term) showed stress rising and sleep, conversation and activity falling as workload rose (https://studentlife.cs.dartmouth.edu). A 2026 "course digital twin" reports only preliminary results (https://doi.org/10.3390/computers15060338). A "campus digital twin" for anxiety is a proof of concept on synthetic data (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13534086/). Evidence is thin. Our "twin" is a running personal baseline with no prediction, which is the defensible version of this idea.

## 3. What moves student wellbeing at population level

Loneliness and belonging. ACHA-NCHA Spring 2025 undergraduates (N=47,194, 96 schools, 10% response): 48.5% screened positive for loneliness (UCLA-3), 67.2% agree they belong, 51.1% agree their school prioritizes student wellbeing, serious psychological distress 20.0%, high stress 25.3%, 36.0% used mental health services in 12 months (https://www.acha.org/wp-content/uploads/NCHA-IIIb_SPRING_2025_UNDERGRADUATE_REFERENCE_GROUP_INSTITUTIONAL_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.pdf). Belonging predicts persistence, engagement and mental health at 4-year schools in a national sample (Gopalan and Brady 2020, https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10280870). A one-hour belonging exercise raised Black students' GPA and self-reported health over three years, N=92 (Walton and Cohen 2011, https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Study/81609).

Financial stress and food. ACHA Spring 2025: 48.0% had problems with finances in 12 months, 46.6% any food insecurity, 21.2% very low food security (ACHA URL above). Hope Center 2023-24 (74,350 students, 91 colleges, not nationally representative, 72% at two-year schools): 41% food insecure, 48% housing insecure, 59% at least one (https://hope.temple.edu/sites/hope/files/media/document/Hope%20Student%20Basic%20Needs%20Survey%20Report%202023-24.pdf). At Rutgers New Brunswick, Cuite's 2019 survey of 8,000+ students found 36.9% of undergraduates and 32.1% of graduate students food insecure, with pantry use rising from 0.3% to 3.1% (https://www.rutgers.edu/news/rutgers-makes-progress-addressing-student-food-insecurity; report at https://scholarship.libraries.rutgers.edu/esploro/outputs/report/Basic-needs-insecurity-among-RutgersNew-Brunswick/991031886292404646). A later figure of 31.2% circulates for 2023 but I could not reach its primary page; do not use it.

Sleep regularity. In 61 Harvard undergraduates, sleep regularity correlated with grades (r=0.37) with no difference in average duration; correlational only (Phillips et al. 2017, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-03171-4). ACHA lists sleep difficulties as hurting academics for roughly a quarter of undergraduates and stress for about 38% (section D of the ACHA PDF; the table parses poorly, verify page 5 before quoting).

Physical activity. A 59-study meta-analysis found activity interventions reduced undergraduate anxiety (SMD -0.88) and depression (SMD -0.73) but with high heterogeneity and very low GRADE certainty (https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/80371 lists the umbrella review; the Health Promotion International review is summarized at https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12673509/). Treat the sizes as upper bounds.

## 4. Design principles from the literature

Engagement decays fast. Across 93 popular mental health apps, median 15-day retention was 3.9% and 30-day 3.3%; tracker and peer-support apps retained best, breathing apps worst (Baumel et al. 2019, https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567/). Design for the second visit, not the download.

Apps help a little, and features matter. 176 RCTs: g=0.28 for depression, 0.26 for anxiety; CBT, mood monitoring and chatbot features were associated with larger effects (Linardon et al. 2024, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10785982/). College-specific reviews find many studies at moderate to severe risk of bias (Lattie et al. 2019, https://www.jmir.org/2019/7/e12869/).

Self-monitoring with feedback raises self-awareness. In 114 adolescents, mood self-monitoring increased emotional self-awareness, which in turn lowered depressive symptoms (Kauer et al. 2012, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22732135). This is the evidence for the personal trend page.

Just-in-time adaptive interventions. Nahum-Shani et al. define JITAIs as delivering "the right amount of support at the right moment" based on changing internal and contextual state, and warn most were built with little evidence (https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/just-in-time-adaptive-interventions-jitais-in-mobile-health-key-c/). Our nudges fit the frame only if each one names a trigger, a decision rule and a measured response.

Nudges from analytics did not work. Three UK RCTs in 2024-25 (UEA, Northumbria, Staffordshire) found that nudging students flagged by analytics toward wellbeing services "had no measurable impact" on engagement or service uptake, and the analytics flags barely overlapped with survey-identified low wellbeing (TASO 2026, https://taso.org.uk/libraryitem/report-improving-student-wellbeing-using-analytics/). Nudges should come from the student's own signals and consent, never from a staff-side risk flag.

Fear framing. The meta-analytic evidence does not say fear appeals backfire; Tannenbaum et al. 2015 found them effective, strongest with efficacy statements (https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2015/10/fear-based-appeals). Our no-fear rule rests on ScarletWell's strengths-based model and tone, not on a claim that fear fails; say it that way.

Students want to be asked. Students supported analytics for mental health only if data were used for the stated purpose, anonymised, with visible benefit (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12316473/). When Northumbria asked at enrolment, 13,122 students consented, 61% rising to 70% (https://wonkhe.com/blogs/data-can-help-predict-where-students-are-struggling-will-wellbeing/).

## 5. Ten things to include, prioritized

1. Partial check-in, any dimension, any day. Evidence: time is the top treatment barrier (Healthy Minds); 30-day all-items cadence is what PULSE8 keeps. Feasibility: done in `app/src/data/inventory.ts`.
2. Strength-first feedback with one step and one place. Evidence: Nod RCT; ScarletWell's strengths model. Feasibility: template keyed by dimension and band, Bedrock polish optional.
3. Fit-first resource ranking with the weights shown. Evidence: Big White Wall RCT failed on unexplained value; students want visible benefit. Feasibility: `weights.yaml` plus a "how we rank" sheet.
4. Personal baseline trend with plain words, no score colors. Evidence: Kauer 2012 self-monitoring; tracker apps retain best (Baumel). Feasibility: EWMA on device, mirrored in engine.
5. Event-aware next steps from the Wellness Calendar. Evidence: ScarletWell's own calendar and grant activities are the supply; loneliness 48.5%. Feasibility: `wellness_events.json`, 7-day window.
6. Financial and food resources ranked up where the gap is. Evidence: 46.6% food insecurity nationally, 36.9% at Rutgers NB. Feasibility: campus flag plus findhelp deep links.
7. Opt-in aggregate with k=5 suppression and a dated action log. Evidence: JED dose-response; grant teams must evaluate. Feasibility: `aggregate/` already enforces k.
8. Wearable sleep shown only to the student, with an association sentence. Evidence: Phillips 2017 regularity; thin digital-twin evidence means no prediction. Feasibility: CSV import plus OAuth; engine stores nothing.
9. Explicit consent screen with purpose, retention and revoke. Evidence: PMC12316473; Northumbria uptake. Feasibility: one page, two toggles.
10. A one-line "why this nudge" with its trigger. Evidence: JITAI design rules; TASO null result for opaque nudges. Feasibility: nudge object carries `trigger` and `source`.

Not recommended this weekend: staff-side risk flags (TASO), a chatbot as the primary surface (engagement decay; PULSE8 already does this), or any predictive model.

## 6. Privacy and governance

FERPA and HIPAA. The joint ED and HHS guidance (updated December 2019) says records covered by FERPA are generally not covered by HIPAA; at postsecondary institutions, treatment records made and used only for treatment are excluded from "education records" until disclosed for another purpose (https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/ferpa-hipaa/index.html; https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/resources/joint-guidance-application-ferpa-and-hipaa-student-health-records). A wellness check-in run by Student Affairs is neither treatment nor a covered-entity transaction, so HIPAA does not apply by default; it becomes a FERPA question once a NetID is attached. Keep the demo NetID-free, and route any clinician sharing through Student Health's own records.

Small cells. PTAC's disclosure avoidance FAQ says aggregation does not remove risk and that each agency must set its threshold and use complementary suppression (https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/sites/default/files/resource_document/file/FAQs_disclosure_avoidance_0.pdf). ACHA tells institutions to "consider a minimum cell size" before publishing their own reports (ACHA PDF above). k-anonymity means each row is indistinguishable from k-1 others on quasi-identifiers (Sweeney 2002, https://epic.org/wp-content/uploads/privacy/reidentification/Sweeney_Article.pdf). Our k=5 matches the Data Quality Campaign's worked example; some agencies use 10 (https://dataqualitycampaign.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/DQC-N-size-paper-FINAL.pdf). Campus by level by week by dimension will often fall below 5 at Camden; suppress and say so.

Consent. Jisc's guidance separates collection from intervention: acting on an individual's data needs explicit consent, and consent-only datasets leave holes (https://analytics.jiscinvolve.org/wp/2017/02/16/consent-for-learning-analytics-some-practical-guidance-for-institutions/). Our design avoids the hole by never acting on anyone from the aggregate. Wearable data has no university-specific norm I could find; treat it as the most sensitive class and keep it on device.

## What is thin

No independent evaluation exists for YOU at College, Mantra, Togetherall in US colleges, Ohio State's app, WellTrack or Wolverine Wellness. The digital twin literature is prototypes and synthetic data. ACHA sleep percentages and the 2023 Rutgers food insecurity figure were not verifiable from here. The MyRutgers widget's live contents are behind login.
