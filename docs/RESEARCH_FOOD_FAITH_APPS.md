# Research: food, dining, faith and cultural resources at Rutgers, and cycle-app data export paths

Compiled 2026-10-11 for ScarletWell live (Rutgers Health Hack 2026). Every fact carries the URL it was read from. Where a page is labelled for a past term, that label is stated. Where nothing is published, that is stated. No PHI, no individual data. Nothing here is a number for the UI until it is also entered in `docs/EVIDENCE.md` with its source.

Fetch note: rutgers.edu, google.com and most vendor help sites were blocked by the session's egress proxy for direct fetch, so pages were read through a remote fetch service. Quotes are verbatim from the fetched page text.

## A. Free and low-cost food at Rutgers (New Brunswick, Newark, Camden)

### A.1 Rutgers Student Food Pantry and Basic Needs Center, New Brunswick

- Location: College Avenue Student Center, 126 College Ave, New Brunswick, NJ 08901, Room 112. The pantry sits inside the Basic Needs Center. Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/food-assistance/food-and-mobile-pantry and https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/basic-needs-center/basic-needs-faqs
- Phone: (848) 932-FOOD (3663). Email: basicneeds@echo.rutgers.edu. Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/basic-needs-center/basic-needs-faqs
- Fall 2026 hours of operation, as published on the pantry page (labelled "Fall 2026 Hours of Operations"):
  - Monday: 10am to 1pm (appointment necessary)
  - Tuesday: 12pm to 4pm (appointment necessary), 4pm to 4:45pm (walk-ins allowed)
  - Wednesday: 12pm to 6pm (appointment necessary), 6pm to 6:45pm (walk-ins allowed)
  - Thursday: 12pm to 6pm (appointment necessary), 6pm to 6:45pm (walk-ins allowed)
  - Friday: 12pm to 4pm (appointment necessary), 4pm to 4:45pm (walk-ins allowed)
  - Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/food-assistance/food-and-mobile-pantry
  - Caution: the FAQ page on the same site still states Monday 10am to 2pm and walk-ins "T/F - 5 pm and W/TH - 7 pm", which is the older Spring 2026 pattern. Treat the pantry page's Fall 2026 block as current and the FAQ as stale. FAQ source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/basic-needs-center/basic-needs-faqs
- Appointment system: PantrySoft. "All students must make an appointment in the PantrySoft system ahead of time if they wish to visit between the hours of 12pm-4pm (M-F), unless they are a first-time visitor. Appointments can be made for the current week starting on Sundays at noon." Tablets are available in the pantry to register. Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/food-assistance/food-and-mobile-pantry
- First visit: "If you're visiting the food pantry for the first time, no appointment is necessary, however, you will need to complete a short intake form." Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/basic-needs-center/basic-needs-faqs
- Visit limit: one visit per week across the pantry, the Knight's Pantry lockers and the mobile pantry. Most items limited to two per visit. One reusable bag is given on the first visit. Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/food-assistance/food-and-mobile-pantry and https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/basic-needs-center/basic-needs-faqs
- Eligibility: all Rutgers New Brunswick students with a valid Rutgers ID. Emergency aid funds are New Brunswick only; Camden students are pointed to the Chancellor's Student Emergency Fund. Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/food-assistance and https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/basic-needs-center/basic-needs-faqs
- Stock: pantry staples, ready-to-eat items, beverages, personal care including tampons, household supplies, a "Cool Zone" (yogurt, milk, dairy, fruit, vegetables, cheese) and a "Freezer Zone" (meals, meat, veggies). Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/food-assistance/food-and-mobile-pantry
- Published usage: "Total Visits (2024-2025 Academic Year): Approximately 9,600" and "Unique Student Visitors: Over 1,700". The pantry opened Fall 2016. Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/food-assistance
- Off-Campus Living & Community Initiatives office: 39 Union Street, New Brunswick, NJ 08901, 848-932-5500, RUoffcampus@echo.rutgers.edu. Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/food-assistance

### A.2 Knight's Pantry Basic Needs Box (after-hours lockers), New Brunswick

- Pickup lockers at College Ave, Busch, Douglass and Livingston Student Centers. Order through PantrySoft, select "After Hours Knight's Pantry Basic Needs Box", pick a date and time, pick up to 15 non-perishable items, scan the QR code on the locker. Orders must be placed at least a day before pickup, no weekend pickup, and the order counts as the week's pantry visit. Locker access depends on student center building hours. Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/food-assistance/food-and-mobile-pantry

Requests through PantrySoft by Tuesday at 5 pm. Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/food-assistance/food-and-mobile-pantry

### A.5 SNAP help at Rutgers

- New Brunswick: the Basic Needs Center page says the center will "offer guidance on enrolling in programs like SNAP and meal plan scholarships." Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/basic-needs-center
- The SNAP page lists student eligibility rules (age 18 to 49, full-time student, not on a meal plan for most meals, plus one qualifying condition such as 20 hours of paid work per week or federal work-study), gross monthly income limits ($2,148 for a household of 1, $2,904 for 2, $3,660 for 3), a link to the NJ SNAP Student Eligibility Verification Form, and a referral to the Community Food Bank of New Jersey for application help. It notes SNAP is only available to U.S. citizens or legal permanent residents. Source: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/food-assistance/supplemental-nutrition-assistance-program-snap
- Newark: pantry+ lists "SNAP Assistance" among its services. Source: https://studentaffairs.newark.rutgers.edu/pantryrun
- Camden: no campus SNAP navigator page was found. The Camden pantry page refers to the OSHE 2022-2023 New Jersey Food Security Survey ("40% of survey respondents qualified as food insecure"). Source: https://wellnesscenter.camden.rutgers.edu/?p=342

### A.11 Published data on pantry demand by day of week or week of term

- Rutgers: no day-of-week or week-of-term breakdown is published for any of the three campus pantries. New Brunswick publishes only annual totals (about 9,600 visits, over 1,700 unique students, AY 2024-2025). Camden publishes only a weekly average (over 200 students per week). Newark publishes no counts. Sources: https://ruoffcampus.rutgers.edu/basic-needs/food-assistance and https://wellnesscenter.camden.rutgers.edu/?p=342
- Swipe Out Hunger's 2023 campus pantry survey (355 pantries) covers sourcing, distribution, programming, staffing and finances. It does not report visit timing by day or week. Source: https://swipehunger.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Campus-Food-Pantries-Insights-From-a-2023-Survey.pdf
- Swipe Out Hunger's 2022 summary of its surveys (352 campuses) reports counts served (over 153,000 people) and infrastructure (71% have fridges), not timing. Source: https://swipehunger.org/pantryhungerdata/
- The Hope Center's 2020 evaluation of Swipe Out Hunger implementation (45 sites) covers program design and staffing, not when swipes or pantries are used. Source: https://hope.temple.edu/sites/hope/files/media/document/SOH_Implementation.pdf
- Swipe Out Hunger notes demand around campus closures: Boise State's pantry supported over 1,500 students in October 2022 alone and runs break meal kits; University of Arkansas's pantry serves more than 1,200 families per month. Source: https://swipehunger.org/campusbreaks/
- The only peer-reviewed timing statement found: a 2022 Nutrients paper on a Midwest campus pantry (logs August 2020 to May 2022) states that "the busiest months in pantry use tended to be midway to late in the semester", citing an earlier study. The full text was not retrievable through the proxy; the abstract page is https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/14/22/4876 (doi 10.3390/nu14224876). Treat as a lead, not a UI number, until the full text is read.
- Single-campus examples of week and season patterns (not day of week):
  - UMass Amherst Campus Pantry: 27,538 visits in its first year; busiest month April (50,000 pounds); "the pantry receives the most traffic during the academic year with dips during the winter and summer breaks"; single busiest day Thursday Dec 4, 2025 (222 households) after a snow closure earlier that week. Open Tuesday and Thursday noon to 7 p.m., Friday and Saturday noon to 4 p.m. Sources: https://www.umass.edu/news/article/campus-pantry-marks-one-year-serving-umass-community and https://www.umass.edu/news/article/new-campus-food-pantry-serves-over-2300-first-months
  - University of Minnesota Nutritious U: 10,251 visits in fall 2025, a 62% increase over fall 2024; open two days a week 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Source: https://twin-cities.umn.edu/news-events/partnering-ensure-food-security-students
- Conclusion for the engine: there is no published day-of-week demand curve for Rutgers or for campus pantries generally. Any "busy day" hint in the app must be labelled as inferred from published hours (for example, Rutgers NB walk-in windows exist only Tuesday to Friday) and not as measured demand.

## B. Dining halls and dietary accommodations

### B.1 Rutgers New Brunswick Dining Services (food.rutgers.edu)

- Dietitians: "Our two expert registered dietitians, Peggy Policastro and Marina Vineis, are available to help students meet their dietary medical needs while on campus." Contact nutrition@dining.rutgers.edu. Source: https://food.rutgers.edu/node/14
- Vegetarian and vegan: "Rutgers Dining Services offers a variety of food items which are naturally vegan and vegetarian as well as specialty vegan and vegetarian items in all of their dining operations." Daily menus with ingredients at food.rutgers.edu under each location's "daily menu". Source: https://food.rutgers.edu/node/14
- Gluten free: naturally gluten-free foods plus specialty gluten-free options "for students with a documented medical need." Source: https://food.rutgers.edu/node/14
- Allergen program: step one is the Medical and Dietary Consideration Form emailed to nutrition@dining.rutgers.edu, followed by a dietitian meeting and an individual nutrition care plan. The Spring 2025 Nutrition Health & Safety Plan states dietitians "enforce accurate food labelling of all food items listed on the online dining hall menus; this includes nutrition facts, ingredients, and allergens" and consider "Vegan, Vegetarian, Gluten Free, Halal, Kosher, and Allergy-Free" diets in menu planning. Sources: https://food.rutgers.edu/node/14 and https://food.rutgers.edu/sites/default/files/pdf/ds-nutrition-safety-and-health-plan-sp2025.pdf
- Text-to-Order: a pre-order program "specifically for students who have the most severe dietary needs"; students pick from online menus and text the order to their dining hall; the nutrition team "contacted over 1,600 students with special dietary needs" in one year. Source: https://studentaffairs.rutgers.edu/news/text-order
- FARE listing: ingredient and Top 9 allergen information online and at point of service via NutriSlice digital labels; pre-order meals available after the Medical & Dietary Considerations form. Source: https://college.foodallergy.org/node/6061
- Cross-contact warning from the Fall 2025 dining newsletter: "Assume all food served, whether on a buffet or from a cook-to-order station, may contain one of the top nine food allergens." Source: https://food.rutgers.edu/sites/default/files/food-allergies_fall-25.pdf
- Halal: "Although Rutgers Dining Services is not a kosher or halal facility, each dining hall offers select pre-made certified kosher meals and halal meats upon request." The religious diets flyer: "All fresh chicken used in recipes at the Busch, Neilson, and Livingston Dining Halls and The Atrium is halal certified. This includes chicken breast, rotisserie chicken, chicken thighs, and chicken strips." Processed chicken items (fingers, nuggets, wings) are not halal unless labelled. Sources: https://food.rutgers.edu/node/14 and https://food.rutgers.edu/sites/default/files/halal-kosher_flyer.pdf
- Daily Targum, 2024-11-20: "all fresh chicken served at Rutgers dining locations is now halal", a change made "in response to requests from leaders within our Muslim Student Organizations" (Policastro). There is no dedicated halal station; the change covers the fresh chicken supply. Source: https://dailytargum.com/article/2024/11/rutgers-dining-halls-pivot-to-serving-exclusively-halal-fresh-chicken
- Kosher: prepackaged certified kosher entrees on request from a chef or manager in each dining hall (kosher certified fish, beef, chicken, vegetarian). Source: https://food.rutgers.edu/sites/default/files/halal-kosher_flyer.pdf
- Kosher retail (since March 2023): Cafe West (College Ave), Douglass Cafe, Cook Cafe, Kilmer's Market (Livingston) and Woody's Cafe (Busch) sell kosher sandwiches made at the Chabad House and certified by its in-house rabbi; Chabad subsidizes the cost. Source: https://dailytargum.com/article/2023/04/rutgers-dining-services-adds-kosher-options-to-5-on-campus-dining-retail
- Rutgers Dining lists a "Kosher Meal Option" on its meal plan information; the Jewish Studies department links to it. Source: https://jewishstudies.rutgers.edu/academics/undergraduate/jewish-life-at-rutgers
- Hillel kosher dining: "There is a full-service kosher dairy restaurant in Hillel, Bistro 70, where students can purchase kosher meals or use Knight Express points through Rutgers Dining Services to acquire meals." Hillel offers free Shabbat and holiday meals. Source: https://www.hillel.org/college/rutgers-university-new-brunswick/
- Chabad kosher meal plan (separate from the university, served at Chabad House): 2026-2027 plans are Complete 255 ($3,440 per semester), Traditional 225 ($3,285), Flex 160 ($2,860), Part-Time 120 ($2,200) and Dinner 80 ($1,580). Rutgers housing residents on a Chabad plan can be exempted from the Rutgers plan (first and second years need Complete 255 or Traditional 225). "All Shabbat meals are free and do not count toward your dining plan." Source: https://chabadnj.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Meal-Plan-Options-2026-2027.pdf
- Ramadan and holiday accommodations: no Rutgers Dining page on Ramadan, suhoor, iftar or Passover was found as of 2026-10-11. The published regular hours give the practical picture: Busch Dining Hall takeout dinner Mon to Thu 5:00 PM to 12:00 AM; Livingston Dining Commons takeout Mon to Thu 11:00 AM to 12:00 AM; Neilson takeout Mon to Thu 11:00 AM to 12:00 AM; The Atrium Mon to Thu 7:00 AM to 12:00 AM; dine-in halls close 9:00 PM weekdays and 8:00 PM weekends. Source: https://food.rutgers.edu/hours-of-operation
- The CILRU Ramadan Guide 2026 (hosted by Rutgers Student Support) gives the 2026 dates (began evening of Feb 17, 2026; Eid March 20, 2026), suhur around 4am to 5am, iftar after sunset about 6pm, and advises staff to avoid scheduling 5pm to 6pm; it says iftar is usually at the Cook Student Center MPR. Source: https://studentsupport.rutgers.edu/sites/default/files/pdf/cilru-ramadan-guide-2026.pdf
- A 2022 Daily Targum column documented the gap: weekend dining halls closed at 8 p.m., less than 30 minutes after sunset, and only 2 of 4 halls offered takeout after 8 p.m.; it cites roughly 5,000 Muslim students. Source: https://dailytargum.com/article/2022/04/ruocco-rutgers-dining-must-better-accommodate-muslim-students-during-ramadan
- Sick meals: a student who is ill can designate a pick-up person to swipe and collect a to-go meal from a dining hall manager. Source: https://food.rutgers.edu/meal-plans
- Meal plan rates Fall 2026 to Spring 2027 (New Brunswick): Scarlet Unlimited $3,918 per semester, 255 Plan $3,779, 210 Plan $3,532, 150 Plan $3,337, 100 Plan $2,114, 75 Plan $1,642, 50 Plan $1,220. Source: https://food.rutgers.edu/meal-plans

Practical conclusion: the two paths that cover most students are an Apple Health export.xml (iPhone) and a CSV or JSON file (Natural Cycles CSV, Clue JSON, Flo JSON, Fitbit Takeout). A web importer should accept a ZIP or XML from Apple Health and parse HKCategoryTypeIdentifierMenstrualFlow records for period start dates, plus a generic "one date per line" CSV fallback. Oura and Stardust have no direct cycle export; both sync to Apple Health. No vendor offers a browser-usable API for cycle data without partner approval.

### D.2 Google Calendar read access for a hackathon app

- Scopes: `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.readonly` ("See and download any calendar you can access using your Calendar"), `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events.readonly` ("View events on all your calendars"), `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events.owned.readonly` ("See the events on Google calendars you own"), `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.freebusy` ("View your availability in your calendars"). Google advises choosing the narrowest scope. Source: https://developers.google.com/calendar/api/auth
- Sensitivity classification: Google says sensitive scopes "have a sensitive indicator on the Google Cloud Console's OAuth consent screen configuration page" and does not publish the tier per scope in its docs. A third-party guide (Nylas, updated 2026-10-07) tables `calendar.readonly` as non-sensitive, `calendar.events.readonly` as sensitive, and `calendar` and `calendar.events` as restricted. Treat the tier as something to confirm in the Console when the scope is added. Sources: https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/scopes and https://developer.nylas.com/docs/cookbook/calendar/events/list-events-google/
- Testing mode: "Projects configured with a publishing status of Testing are limited to up to 100 test users listed in the OAuth consent screen." Test users see a warning before authorizing. "Authorizations by a test user will expire seven days from the time of consent", including refresh tokens. The only exception is apps that request just name, email and profile (openid, userinfo.email, userinfo.profile). Source: https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945
- Production: after "Publish app", apps requesting sensitive or restricted scopes face verification; unverified apps show an "Unverified apps" warning and are subject to a lifetime user cap on unapproved sensitive or restricted scopes that "cannot be reset or changed". Source: https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945
- Verification requirements (if ever submitted): homepage on a verified domain, privacy policy on that domain and linked from the consent screen, domain ownership verified in Search Console, Google branding rules, and for sensitive or restricted scopes a demo video of the OAuth flow. Apps using only non-sensitive scopes need no verification, only optional brand verification for name and logo. Sources: https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464321 and https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/9110914
- Implication for the demo: keep the Google Cloud project in Testing, add judges and teammates as test users (up to 100), request `calendar.events.readonly` or `calendar.readonly` only, and expect the token to lapse after seven days. The existing export-file path (ICS from Google Calendar, Outlook, Canvas, WebReg) stays as the no-OAuth fallback per `docs/SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` 2.9.
